# 18 — Demo Script and Defense

## Recommended end-to-end demonstration
1. Owner logs in.
2. Owner creates a diary/lecture entry.
3. Show original preservation and a versioned cleaned copy.
4. AI extracts candidate person/topic/relationship.
5. Owner approves the candidate.
6. Approved memory appears on the Neural Canvas with category color.
7. Open node and navigate to source entry.
8. Ask BayMax a question; show grounded answer and dated citation.
9. Demonstrate approved communication preference affecting response style.
10. Create a reminder proposal and show confirmation before execution.
11. Record a short manual voice note, transcribe and review it.
12. Export a conversation or memory.
13. Configure a legacy policy and validate syntax.
14. Show Prolog access tests: pre-release deny, allow, explicit deny override.
15. Simulate release.
16. Beneficiary logs in and sees only permitted memories.
17. Ask beneficiary AI a question and show citations from authorized records only.
18. Attempt unauthorized access and show denial.

## Defense questions
- Why is Prolog used instead of nested if-statements?
- Which parts demonstrate OOP, functional and logic paradigms?
- Why preserve original entries and use append-only versions?
- What does a hash chain detect, and what does it not guarantee?
- How does the system prevent unauthorized records reaching an LLM?
- How are conflicting allow/deny rules resolved?
- How does the parser handle malformed policy?
- How do you verify AI-generated code?
- How does owner approval prevent false memories?
- How does the system behave when Prolog or the AI provider is unavailable?

## Demo safety
Use fictional accounts and fictional memories. Use a simulated release event. Do not present BayMax as a legally valid will or as a real posthumous identity.
